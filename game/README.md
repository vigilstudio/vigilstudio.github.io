# The Long Watch

A multiplayer game that grows one small piece per run: an island at night and the people who keep its
lanterns. Right now (milestone 32, carrying the flame together: a flame taken from a lit beacon can be carried, handed on and used to light another beacon) players pick one of six lantern rooms (and can ask which one looks busiest, a guess from numbers only), walk the seeded island with its seven landmarks
(old beacons, a ruined lighthouse, a jetty, a well) and a minimap, in light that changes through an eight-hour night (a blue dusk and a grey dawn at its two ends),
see everyone else in the same room move (sound, meaning footsteps and the music stone's notes, is off by default), and talk with 5 gestures and 16 preset phrases (ids only, never typed text), with arrows
at the screen edge pointing to players who are off screen. They gather driftwood, reeds and oil berries, give them (and the wicks and lanterns they craft) to each other, swap one item for one item with a nearby player (each side sets its item aside first; nothing is copied), and craft wicks and amber, jade or azure lanterns whose light reaches further for everyone nearby. A music stone beside the well plays eight preset notes (a number each, nothing typed, heard only with Sound on); when three players play its tune of the night together within a beat it glows for everyone and counts as help. Mist and rain drift over the island and the sky strip darkens when shades come (all drawn by each browser from the seed and the clock); in mist the names of players farther than 6 tiles fade (they stay on the minimap), and in rain the shades move a fifth slower. Walkers leave fading footprints on sand and meadow and a spreading ring in shallow water, so a group's path can be read for a few seconds (drawn by each browser from positions it already has, nothing sent; the rings hold still with reduced motion). Figures walk with a stepping stride and a swinging lantern, and bow, wave and cheer with their own poses (each browser draws them alone; still with reduced motion). Trees, rocks and the landmarks throw soft shadows away from nearby lanterns, lit beacons and the lit lighthouse, and beacon flames and the lighthouse lamp flicker (still shadows and steady flames with reduced motion). The island is alive: glinting water, shore foam, swaying trees, fireflies round lanterns and a moon that crosses the sky strip as the night goes on (all of it holds still with reduced motion). Each day (UTC) the keeper writes three small notes from the seed and the date (gather some of one thing, make wicks or amber lanterns, burn fuel in one named beacon) that everyone in a lantern works on together; a note needs at least two players. At night, slow hooded shades drift in off the sea, circle a landmark and snuff the lantern of anyone they reach for 14 seconds; they hurt no one, and light drives them off, but a shade is only driven off when at least two players have put light on it, and a friend's light holds it back and relights you. When all four old beacons in a lantern burn at once, the ruined lighthouse lights for everyone there until the lantern empties, and shades keep out of its beam (worked out by each browser from the beacons, nothing extra is sent; the shades' places rely on players' device clocks agreeing). If another player's device clock differs from yours by 10 seconds or more, a plain note names them (shades, weather and the keeper's day all assume clocks agree). Fishing: cast from the jetty or a shore (F) and press again when a fish bites to catch lamp oil (3 fuel in a beacon, can be given); fish come and go with the seed and the clock (nothing about them is sent) and two players with floats within 4 tiles of each other draw a shoal in for both. Helping others (relighting a friend, driving off a shade together, a gift that arrives, lighting a beacon or finishing a keeper's note together) earns points toward a lamplighter rank, shown as a title and gold marks on your lantern; it is decoration only. Peer to peer over WebRTC (Trystero, signalling through public Nostr relays and BitTorrent trackers, with a few spares from a list in the code if fewer than two are open after 10 seconds, and the connected path shown on the page; the owner has played it online on an earlier version; this milestone is
untested), no
server, no accounts, no text between players.
