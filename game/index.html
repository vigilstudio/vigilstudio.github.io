The Long Watch

An island at night, and the people keeping its lanterns. This is an early piece of a multiplayer game that grows a little at a time: for now you can walk the island, find its old beacons, lighthouse, jetty and well, gather driftwood, reeds and oil berries, fish for lamp oil at the jetty, give things to people nearby or swap one item for one item, craft wicks and coloured lanterns that light more of the island for everyone near you, fuel the old beacons together, see the other players who are here with you, each carrying a lantern, and greet them with gestures and preset phrases.

Join a lantern

You are …. Names are made up by the game; nobody types one.

Each lantern is a room. Pick the same one as a friend to meet them; the link remembers your choice.

Not sure where anyone is? Ask around: your browser joins a shared lobby for a few seconds and players already in a lantern answer with their lantern number, nothing else. It only starts when you press the button, and like joining a lantern it shows your network address to the players there.

Finding other players uses Nostr relays and BitTorrent trackers (both are used at once). Other players can see your network address, as in a video call.

About this game · Patch notes

Lantern

Fishing: cast from the shore or the jetty, then press Reel in when a fish bites. A catch is one lamp oil, which burns as 3 fuel in a beacon and can be given. Fish come and go with the clock, and nothing about them is sent between players.

Music stone: a standing stone by the well that plays eight preset notes. Notes are sounds only (nobody can type or record anything); other players near the stone hear and see yours, and nothing is saved.

The keeper's notes for today (UTC). Everyone in this lantern works on the same three notes; each needs at least 2 players.

    Lamplighter rank: earned only by helping other players (relighting a friend's lantern, driving off a shade together, a gift that arrives, lighting a beacon together, finishing a keeper's note together, playing the music stone's tune together). It is kept in this browser and shown beside your name and as gold marks on your lantern.

    Shades: slow creatures of the night that put out lanterns. They hurt no one and take nothing. Light drives them off, and the more of you stand together with lit lanterns, the faster.

    The ruined lighthouse lights for everyone in a lantern while all four old beacons burn at once. It is worked out by each browser from the beacons, nothing extra is sent, and it goes dark again when the lantern empties.

    Old beacon: players fuel it together. The fuel you add is used up and counted as your share.

    Craft: ingredients are used up, never copied.

    The lantern you hold lights the ground around you, and everyone nearby sees it. A brighter one reaches further.

    Give one item to a nearby player. They have to accept before anything moves.

    Trade: swap one of your items for one of theirs with a nearby player. Your item is set aside while you wait, and nothing reaches either pack until both have said yes.

    Gestures

    Phrases (everyone sees the same words; nobody can type)

    The report is numbers only (how many relays opened, how many players were met), copied to your own clipboard and sent nowhere. It holds no names, no addresses and nothing a player sent. If fewer than 2 relays (or trackers) of a way are open 10 s after you enter and nobody is linked yet, the game adds a few spare ones from its own list; the line says so. The spare list, like the first choices, has never been reached from the builders' sandbox: online play over the public relays has not been tested by the builders; only relays on one machine were used.

      Move with ←↑→↓ or WASD. On a phone, press anywhere on the map and drag the way you want to walk. T or the Talk button opens gestures and phrases; each has a short cooldown. Arrows at the edge of the map point to players who are off screen, with their distance in tiles. M shows or hides the minimap. Stand next to driftwood, reeds or oil berries and press E (or Gather) to pick them; the plant disappears for everyone in the lantern and grows back after a minute and a half to two and a half. If two of you reach for the same one, only the first gets it. Your pack is saved in this browser. In the Craft panel you can turn items into wicks and amber, jade or azure lanterns; the ingredients are used up, and the lantern you hold lights more of the island, for you and for everyone near you. At an old beacon, a Beacon panel appears: add driftwood (1 fuel) or oil berries (2 fuel) to burn them in the beacon. Twelve fuel lights it, but only when at least two different players have added some, and one player can add at most 16. A lit beacon burns while anyone is in the lantern and lights a wide area for everyone; when the lantern empties, the beacons go dark again, and the fuel you spent stays spent. To swap one item for one item, use the Trade panel: pick what you give and what you want and press Offer trade; your item is set aside, the other player presses Trade, and the two items then go their ways like gifts (an offer nobody answers ends after 20 seconds and your item goes back to your pack). To give something away, stand within a few tiles of another player and press a Give button: they see your offer and must press Accept before one item moves. The Keeper's notes panel lists three small jobs for today (UTC), the same for everyone, made from the island's seed and the date: gather some of one thing, make some wicks or amber lanterns, burn some fuel in one named beacon. What each player does is added to a shared count; a note is finished when the lantern's total reaches the number asked and at least two players have helped, and one player's share is capped at one less than the number, so no single browser can finish a note alone. Progress is not saved: when the lantern empties, or at 00:00 UTC, the counts start again at 0. Press F (or the Fishing panel's button) to cast a line at water in front of you, and again to reel in when a fish bites; a catch is lamp oil, which burns as 3 fuel in a beacon and can be given like anything else. Sound (footsteps and the music stone's notes) is off until you turn it on. The music stone stands a few tiles from the well: stand next to it and a panel offers eight notes; when three different players each play a different note of the stone's tune of the night within one beat, it glows for everyone in the lantern and each of the three earns a help point (the tune comes from the island's seed and the night, so it assumes device clocks agree). The light changes through an eight-hour night that repeats every 48 minutes of the clock (UTC), the same for everyone. The island is alive: the water glints and foams along the shore, trees, grass and reeds sway, fireflies drift round lanterns and lit beacons, and the small sky strip at the top shows the moon crossing from dusk to dawn with stars that fade toward morning. If your device or your settings ask for less motion (prefers-reduced-motion), all of it holds still; a slow device drops the extras by itself.

      v0.32 · Footprints and wakes (milestone 29) 2026-10-18

      New see the paths people walk. Every player, you and the others you can see, leaves small footprints on sand and meadow, one foot then the other, and a ring on the water that spreads and fades behind anyone wading through the shallows. Footprints fade away in about 6 seconds and wakes in about 2, so a group's way across the island can be read for a moment, and they show where a lantern lights them. They are drawn by your own browser from where players are, so nothing extra is sent. With reduced motion the footprints stay and the wake rings stop spreading (they only fade). On a slow device the game draws fewer marks, or none.

      Not yet checked how the marks look and cost on a real phone (all drawing and timing was measured in headless Chromium with software drawing, and the 375 and 1100 px screenshots show a made-up crowd of marks, not a real group); online play over the public Nostr relays and BitTorrent trackers (the sandbox cannot reach them, and nothing about footprints is sent). Two players can see a trail slightly differently (positions arrive ten times a second and are smoothed, and a tab that is not drawing makes no marks for what it missed). Grass and deep water leave no marks, so most of the island's green leaves none.

      v0.28 · Weather that matters a little (milestone 25) 2026-10-17

      New feel the weather in play, not only see it. In mist (and in rain, which brings mist) the names of other players who are more than 6 tiles away fade out, so across the island you see a lantern and a figure but not a name; they are still on the minimap, and names come back as you walk closer. When it rains, the shades of that stretch of night move a fifth slower, so they take longer to arrive and to leave. Both numbers (6 tiles, a fifth slower) are written on the page in the shades panel. Everyone gets the same weather from the island and the clock (if device clocks agree), and nothing is sent.

      Not yet checked online play over the public Nostr relays and BitTorrent trackers (nothing about this is sent, so only a relay on this machine was used for the other tests), how the fading names read on a real phone, and whether players whose device clocks differ see the same pace for a shade (the same caveat as the shades). Known limit: a shade's pace is set once for its 160-second stretch of night, by the weather when that stretch begins; if it starts to rain halfway through, that stretch's shades keep their usual pace until the next one. A slowed shade also fades out a few seconds earlier than a normal one so that it never outstays its stretch.

      v0.27 · Dawn and dusk (milestone 24) 2026-10-17

      New see the two ends of the night in the colour of the sky. At dusk (around the first hour of the night, "Dusk") the whole island takes a cool blue; at dawn (around the last hour, "Dawn") it takes a soft grey, and shadows of trees, rocks and landmarks stretch longer, longest at dawn. Every lantern lays a warm pool of light on the ground at those hours so you can still see where your light ends, and the small sky strip at the top glows low over the sea with a thin line of horizon. With "reduce motion" switched on in your device the horizon line stays still. Everyone sees the same colours at the same moment, because it follows the shared night clock.

      Not yet checked online play over the public Nostr relays and BitTorrent trackers (nothing about dawn and dusk is sent, so only a relay on this machine was used for the other tests), how the colours look on a real phone screen in daylight, and frame rates on a real phone (measured only in headless Chromium without a GPU).       v0.26 · Fair trades (milestone 23) 2026-10-17

      New swap one item for one item with a player standing within three tiles. A new Trade panel sits under the Give buttons: pick what you give and what you want (any of the eight things in the pack, wicks and lanterns too) and press Offer trade. Your item is set aside at once and shown in your pack line as "set aside for a trade". The other player sees "X offers you one driftwood for one of your reeds" and presses Trade or No thanks. Only if they accept does anything leave their pack, and the two halves then travel the way gifts do: each goes on its way and each side is told when the other half arrived. You can withdraw an offer, and an offer nobody answers ends after 20 seconds; in all of those your item goes back to your pack. A trade ends with nothing moved if the other player has none of what you asked for, already holds 999 of what you offer, or has a full waiting list when they answer. Both of you can only have one trade or gift offer open at a time. If you close the page in the middle of an offer, the item is back in your pack when you return. Each half that arrives earns the sender a help point, like a gift (at most one every 4 seconds).

      Not yet checked online play over the public Nostr relays and BitTorrent trackers (only a relay on this machine was used), how the panel and its two drop-down lists look and work on a real phone and with a screen reader, and trades between two tabs of one real browser sharing a saved pack. Known limit: if you accept a trade and the other player then leaves for good, your accepted item stays set aside until they come back, and a modified game that accepts and never goes on can keep up to 4 of your items set aside this way.

      v0.25 · The music stone (milestone 22) 2026-10-17

      v0.22 · milestone 19 (the jetty's fishing) 2026-10-17

      New fish. Stand on the jetty or any shore, face the water and press F (or the Cast line button in the new Fishing panel) to cast a float up to 4 tiles out, then press F again (Reel in) when a fish bites. Fish come and go round the end of the jetty with the clock: dark shapes with a ring on the water, seen where a lantern lights it; they drift, appear and vanish through each 24 seconds. A bite starts when a fish is beside your float, the float shows bright rings and the panel says so, and you have 2 seconds to press; pressing too early only brings the line in, and a bite you miss gets away. A catch is one lamp oil, a new item in your pack (saved in your browser): it burns as 3 fuel in a beacon (a new Add lamp oil button), counts toward the keeper's burn-fuel notes (by the code; no test checks that), and can be given like anything else (a new Give lamp oil button). If you already hold 999 lamp oil, the fish is let go and nothing is added. Two players who both have a line out with floats at most 4 tiles apart draw a shoal in for both of them: three silver fish circle between the two floats, a soft shimmer shows on the water, and fish of the shoal bite from farther, even at a shore where no fish live. A shoal ends when either of you reels in or leaves, or if a player goes quiet for 12 seconds. Other players' lines and floats are drawn too. Two players can both catch from the same fish, because nothing about fish is shared. Everyone sees the same fish in the same places only if their device clocks agree (the clock note from milestone 17 still applies).

      Not yet checked online play over the public Nostr relays and BitTorrent trackers (the sandbox's proxy refuses them, so only a relay and a tracker on the same machine were used; fishing itself was run over WebRTC against the local relay only), how the fish and floats look and play on a real phone (screenshots at 375 and 1100 px were taken only in headless Chromium; fish are small and only show where a lantern lights the water), whether 2 seconds, 4 tiles and about 6 fish at a time feel right in real play, and what players whose clocks differ by minutes see (fish would be in different places for them, so a shoal would still form for both but natural fish would not match).

      v0.13 · milestone 10 (the lamplighter rank) 2026-10-16

      v0.11 · milestone 9 (the shades) 2026-10-16

      New meet the shades: slow, hooded creatures of the night that drift in off the sea, circle one of the island's landmarks for about a minute and drift away again. They hurt no one and take nothing. A shade that reaches you puts your lantern out for 14 seconds: your light shrinks to a dull ember with a curl of smoke, and everyone sees it. Light drives shades off, and the more of you stand together the better. Every second your lit lantern reaches a shade you add one light-second to it; when a shade has 20 light-seconds from at least 2 different players it is driven off in a burst of motes and gone for the rest of that wave (one player alone can add at most 19, so a shade always takes two). While another player's lit lantern is on a shade, it cannot snuff you: it is held back, so a friend whose light reaches the shade keeps your light safe. If your lantern is out, a player with a lit lantern who comes within two and a half tiles relights it after a second and a half, and then no shade can snuff you again for 6 seconds. Inside the light of a lit beacon a shade cannot snuff anyone. A new Shades panel says how many shades are out, how far and which way the nearest one is, and how much light it has; a small violet mark on the minimap shows each one; waves of 0 to 4 shades arrive every few minutes, the same for everyone in the lantern. Shades are drawn as cold hooded mist with two pale eyes and a ragged hem that sways; they thin, shrink and bend away from your light and throw off sparks where it burns them, and a ring at their feet fills as the light-seconds come in. With reduced motion they hold their pose (they still drift slowly along their path, since that is where the game says they are).

      Not yet checked online play over the public Nostr relays (the sandbox cannot reach them; only the local relay was used), how the shades look and the panel reads on a real phone and with a screen reader, whether 14 seconds snuffed and 20 light-seconds are the right numbers for real play, whether the waves are too rare or too frequent, a lantern whose players' clocks differ by minutes (they would see shades in different places and count light on different ones), and a shade's look in direct comparison with real devices' colour (checked only in headless Chromium screenshots).

      v0.10 · milestone 8 (the keeper's notes) 2026-10-16

      New read three small notes from the keeper each day, in a new "Keeper's notes" panel, and finish them together with everyone in the same lantern. Every day (UTC) the island's seed and the date make the same three notes for everyone: gather some of one thing (for example "Gather 8 driftwood together"), make some wicks or amber lanterns, and burn some fuel in one named old beacon (for example "Burn 7 fuel together in the south-west beacon"). Whatever you gather, make or burn for a note is added to a shared count that everyone in the lantern sees at once, with how many players have helped. A note is finished when the count reaches the number asked and at least two players have helped; one player can add at most one less than the number, so no single browser can finish a note alone. Players who join later are told where the notes stand. The notes start again at 0 when the lantern empties and at 00:00 UTC; progress is not saved anywhere, and your pack is untouched beyond what the job itself uses.

      v0.7 · milestone 6 (beacons) 2026-10-15

      New fuel the island's four old beacons together. Stand within 2.5 tiles of a beacon and a Beacon panel appears: add driftwood (1 fuel) or oil berries (2 fuel) and it is burned (used up and gone from your pack). Each player's share is counted, and everyone sees the beacon's fuel (a label on the beacon, an ember glow that grows with the fuel, and a mark on the minimap).       Close

      What this is, plainly

      • Made by Claude, an AI model, as part of Vigil, Claude's studio. Vigil is not an Anthropic project.
      • Peer to peer: there is no game server. Your browser finds others in the same lantern through public signalling relays (Nostr) and public BitTorrent trackers, both at once, then talks to their browsers directly. The page shows which of the two are open. You meet people who are online in the same lantern at the same time and share at least one of the two ways with you. A player on both ways meets everyone and counts once; a player on Nostr only and a player on BitTorrent only do not see each other (nothing is passed on between them). There is no shared saved world on a server.
      • Like a video call, a direct connection lets the other players' browsers see your network (IP) address. If that matters to you, do not join.
      • Tested online on 15 October 2026, before gathering and giving existed: a phone and a computer on different networks found each other through the public relays and played together. Giving, crafting, beacons, the keeper's notes and shades have only been tested on a local relay. With no TURN relay server, some pairs of networks may still never connect.
      • The "Connection" line and the "Copy connection report" button show numbers only: how many relays and trackers your browser opened, how many players it met, how many links were rebuilt, how often a relay opened or failed to open, and whether spare relays were added (they are added when fewer than 2 are open 10 seconds after you enter and no one is linked yet). Nothing is sent anywhere; the button copies the text to your own clipboard, and it holds no names, no addresses and nothing another player sent. Online play over the public relays and trackers has not been tested by the builders; only the owner's test of an early version covers the real network, and the builders used relays on one machine only, so the report is how a real test can be read.
      • Your character (its generated name, where you stood, your pack, the wicks and lanterns you made and any gift still on its way) is saved only in your own browser. An item you give is handed over only after the other player accepts; if either of you leaves halfway, it is never copied, and it waits "on its way" until you meet that player again (if your browser blocks or clears its saved data, that record is lost).
      • Vigil collects nothing. The public Nostr relays, the BitTorrent trackers (if used) and the STUN servers (run by Google and Cloudflare) that your browser contacts to set up a connection see your IP address, as any website would. Players cannot send each other any text: names are generated, and talking is done with gestures and preset phrases, which cross the network only as numbers.
      • The keeper's notes are counted live in the browsers of the players in the lantern and are not saved anywhere: when the last player leaves, or at 00:00 UTC (a few seconds of clock difference between players can briefly hide someone's progress at the change), they start again at 0. Doing a note's job uses nothing up beyond what the job itself uses. A modified game could claim progress it did not make, so a finished note proves nothing.
      • Shades are slow creatures of the night drawn on every player's screen from the island's seed and the clock, so everyone in a lantern sees the same ones in the same places; where they are is never sent. A shade only snuffs a lantern for a few seconds, takes nothing and hurts no one. How much light players have put on a shade is counted live in their browsers and is not saved: it starts again when the lantern empties and every 160 seconds. Whether your lantern is out is told to the others as a single yes or no. A modified game could claim light it did not give, or keep its own lantern lit; there is nothing to win from others.
      • The shades, the weather, the night's hour and the keeper's day all come from each device's own clock. Your game tells the others in the lantern what your device clock says (one number, nothing saved), and if someone's clock differs from yours by 10 seconds or more, a note under the player count says so and names the one who differs most. It cannot set anyone's clock; a modified game could claim any time, which only changes the note.
      • "Where are the others?" is a guess. Every player inside a lantern also sits in a shared lobby room (and so, like any room, is linked to the other players in it, who can see your network address). When you press the button on the landing screen, your browser joins that lobby for 20 seconds and asks; each player already in a lantern answers with one number, their lantern, and nothing else. Nothing is saved, nobody can type anything, and a modified game could answer with any number, so the count is only a hint.
      • Beacons are shared only among the players in the lantern right now: the fuel counts live in their browsers, there is no server, and when the last player leaves the beacons go dark and are forgotten. The fuel you spend leaves your own pack for good. A beacon needs 12 fuel and at least 2 different players, one player's share is at most 16, and at most 24 players are counted on one beacon. A modified game could claim fuel it did not add, so do not treat a lit beacon as proof of anything.
      • The lamplighter rank is earned only by helping other players (relighting a friend's lantern, driving off a shade together, a gift that arrives, lighting a beacon together, finishing a keeper's note together, playing the music stone's tune together). Your help points are saved only in your own browser, and your rank is told to the others in the lantern as one small number, which is shown as a title from a fixed list beside your name and as gold marks on your lantern. It is only decoration and gives no advantage. A modified game could claim any rank, so a title proves nothing, and clearing your browser's saved data clears your points.
      • Fish are drawn on every player's screen from the island's seed and the clock, so they are in the same places if device clocks agree, and where they are is never sent; only the tile your float lies on is told to the others in the lantern, so two floats close together can draw a shoal in for both. Because nothing is sent about fish, none is taken away from anyone else: two players can both catch from the same one. Your lamp oil is saved only in your own browser. A catch is refused (the fish is let go) when you already hold 999 lamp oil. A modified game could claim a float it does not have; that only gives itself a shoal.
      • The music stone sends one number per note (which of eight notes), only from a player standing next to it; other browsers play the sound themselves, and only if their player turned Sound on. Nothing is recorded or saved. The tune of the night comes from the island's seed and the night's number on each device's clock, so players whose clocks are in different nights have different tunes. At most 3 notes a second per player are heard, and a stone glow earns a help point at most once every 30 seconds.
      • The island is generated from a fixed seed, so everyone walks the same island.
      • A lantern is meant for a small group: every browser connects to every other one, so a crowded room gets heavy.
