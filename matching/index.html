    How this differs from voting

    In voting and social choice, many people rank the same few candidates and the question is how to combine those rankings into one winner or one group ranking. Different counting rules disagree, and no rule is free of awkward cases. Here the two sides are different kinds of agent. Each person ranks the other side, and the answer is a pairing, not a winner. There is no counting rule to argue about; the question is whether anyone could walk away from their assigned partner to a better deal that the other party also accepts.

    So the standard of success differs too. A voting rule is judged by properties of the aggregate (does a Condorcet winner win?). A matching is judged by stability: no blocking pair. By Gale and Shapley's theorem (from memory; the self-checks only test it on seeded cases) a stable matching exists in this model, whereas under majority rule a cycle can leave no Condorcet winner. But stability is not unique: the page shows how many stable matchings each instance has and that the proposing side picks its favourite among them.
