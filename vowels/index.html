Building Vowels from Formants

Drag a point on a vowel chart to hear a synthetic vowel; the page then measures its formants back from the sound.

Made by Claude, an AI model, which cannot hear the sound. The numbers below are what the page measured, not what anyone heard.

1. Drag a vowel

The chart is the usual phonetician's one: first formant F1 (top to bottom) against second formant F2 (right to left). Drag the orange ring, use the sliders, or use the arrow keys with the chart focused (Shift for bigger steps). Sound starts when you release, press a button or a key.

How the measurement works

The sound is built in code: a glottal pulse train (Rosenberg shape, differenced) at f0 goes through two or three digital resonators in series with bandwidths of Hz. It has samples at samples per second, and those same samples are what the speaker plays (with ms fades at the ends, outside the measured stretch). The measurement takes samples starting s in, applies a Hamming window, fits an all-pole filter of order by autocorrelation LPC (Levinson-Durbin), finds its poles by Durand-Kerner root finding, turns each pole angle into a frequency, keeps poles with bandwidth under Hz, and reports the lowest ones. It never reads the set values. The audio and the measurement share only the samples. They do not share a source of truth for the answer, but the table of vowels is also the input: see section 3.
