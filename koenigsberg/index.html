Crossing Each Bridge Once

Draw a graph and this page finds a walk using each edge once, or shows from the odd degrees why none exists.

    2. Why the odd nodes decide it

    Follow a walk that uses each edge once. Each time it passes through a node it comes in on one edge and leaves on another, so it uses edges at that node two at a time. Only the node where the walk starts and the node where it ends can have an odd number of edges left over. So a walk exists only if the graph has 0 or 2 odd nodes, and the edges must also be one connected piece. That is the degree-parity rule, and it is the closed form the checks below test.

    The converse needs a construction, and the page builds one (Hierholzer's method): walk from the start along unused edges until stuck, then splice in the side loops that were left unused. Zero odd nodes gives a closed walk (a circuit); two odd nodes gives an open one that must start at one and finish at the other.

    4. History from memory, weaker

    None of this section was checked against a source in this run; dates and counts may be off.

    • Königsberg (now Kaliningrad, Russia) lay on the river Pregel and had seven bridges joining four areas of land: two banks and two islands. Townspeople wondered whether a walk could cross each bridge once. The layout used on this page is the usual textbook map as I recall it.
    • Euler wrote about it in 1736 (I recall a letter of March 1736 to Giovanni Marinoni). His paper, "Solutio problematis ad geometriam situs pertinentis", carries 1736 in its volume title; I recall it was read in 1735 and printed in 1741. I am not sure which of these years is the one to quote.
    • Euler argued that the walk is impossible for that map and, as I recall, stated the parity condition. I recall that the proof that connectedness plus 0 or 2 odd nodes suffices was published by Carl Hierholzer in 1873, after his death.
    • I recall that bombing in 1944 and later rebuilding changed the bridges, so the real city no longer matches the map. I do not know how many survive.

    5. What is not checked

    • The rule is checked against search on small graphs only: the counts in section 3 are the whole extent. The argument in section 2 is the standard one, but this page does not prove it formally.
    • The drawn graph is limited to nodes, edges, parallel edges per pair and loops on a node, so the search stays quick. The literal listing of edge orders runs only up to edges.
    • Directed edges and weights are not handled; the walk may use an edge in either direction.
    • The page was tested in Node and a script-driven browser, not on a phone or with a screen reader.
