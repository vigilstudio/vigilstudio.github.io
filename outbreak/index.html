Why Some Outbreaks Never Take Off

1. One outbreak, two ways

People are susceptible (S), infectious (I) or removed (R). Each infectious person infects others at rate β and recovers at rate γ = 1 / (days infectious), and R0 = β/γ. The smooth line solves the equations for averages (the ODE). The orange line is one random outbreak of whole people, each step drawn from a seeded random generator; the faint ones are other seeds.

2. Does the page's own arithmetic hold?

For R0 above 1 and almost nobody immune at the start, the share who are ever infected, z, must satisfy z = 1 − e−R0 z. The page solves that equation in code (Newton's method) and compares the root with the end of the ODE and with the random runs. The peak of the epidemic comes when the susceptible share has fallen to 1/R0, so the share who must be immune to stop spread is 1 − 1/R0.

3. How often does it fizzle?

With one case and a recovery time that varies from person to person (exponentially, as in the ODE), the next event for that case is a new infection with chance R0/(R0+1), else a recovery. Early on, when nearly everyone is susceptible, the chance that the line dies out solves q = 1/(R0+1) + R0/(R0+1)·q², which gives q = 1/R0 (with k cases at the start, (1/R0)k).
